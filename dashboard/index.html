🎯 Dual Business Decision Lenses & Problem Formulation

Aligning machine learning objectives with hotel revenue and front-desk operational reality

Primary Lens: Cancellation Risk Prediction & Revenue Mitigation

Stakeholders: Hotel General Managers & Revenue Directors.
Decision Dilemma: Hotel rooms are perishable inventory. Vacant rooms destroy profit, while unguided overbooking incurs $250+ guest walking compensation and brand damage.
Unit of Analysis: A single confirmed booking transaction ($N = 119,390$).
Target & Task: Supervised binary classification predicting $P(\text{is\_canceled}=1)$ + Cost-Sensitive expected value thresholding ($T^* = 0.39$).

Output: Probability + 3-Tier Risk Rating

Secondary Lens: Behavioral Booking & Customer Segmentation

Coherent Justification: Directly strengthens the primary lens because cancellation elasticity differs across traveler archetypes (e.g. business travelers vs family vacationers vs long-lead tour groups).
Methodology: Unsupervised $K$-Means clustering on behavioral features (lead time, stay nights, ADR, special requests, cancellation history).
Output: 4 distinct guest personas with targeted deposit and retention policies.

Output: 4 Customer Personas & Action Playbooks

📊 Key Exploratory Data Analysis & Empirical Patterns

Revealing non-linear drivers, the 'Deposit Paradox', and booking commitment signals

Finding 1: Lead Time Risk Gradient. Cancelled bookings have a mean lead time of 145 days (median: 113d) vs 80 days (median: 49d) for confirmed stays.
Finding 2: The Deposit Type Paradox. Non-refundable bookings exhibit a 99% cancellation rate due to heavy institutional adoption by speculative tour group blocks.
Finding 3: Customer Commitment Signals. Parking space requests reduce cancellation risk to nearly 0.0%, and each special request drops cancellation risk by ~12%.
Finding 4: Property Seasonality. City Hotel cancellations peak in summer months (~44%), while Resort Hotel cancellations remain steadier (~30-33%).
